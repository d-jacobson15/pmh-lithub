Disciplinary Literacy Priority:

Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Overview

Vocabulary is central to how students understand subject content and communicate what they know. Vocabulary can be divided into three tiers.

Explicitly teaching these words, then deliberately revisiting them through reading, discussion and writing, helps students build the language they need to access increasingly complex content and construct precise, subject-appropriate responses.

What is the problem?

When Tier 2 academic words or Tier 3 subject-specific terms are unfamiliar, partially understood or used imprecisely, students can struggle to access new content and express relationships between ideas in subject-appropriate language.

Classroom indicators

What might you notice?

Students whose Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary requires development may:

01

Use simple, repetitive or conversational language when speaking and writing.

02

Produce written responses that lack subject-specific terminology.

03

Understand a concept but struggle to explain it in spoken and written forms.

04

Struggle to understand academic language in questions, instructions and texts.

05

Rely heavily on the wording of notes, textbooks or teachers rather than expressing ideas independently.

Why this matters

The Evidence for Action

A limited academic (Tier 2) and subject-specific (Tier 3) vocabulary is a barrier between what a student knows and what they can demonstrate. Students need vocabulary to:

Access Knowledge

Students need to understand the specialised and technical vocabulary embedded across the secondary curriculum to access and master subject content (EEF, 2024).

Understand Texts & Questions

Reading and comprehending sources of information depends heavily on students understanding the vocabulary within them, with vocabulary knowledge accounting for 50–60% of the variance in reading comprehension (AERO, 2024; Stahl & Nagy, 2006).

Communicate Knowledge

Every subject has its own academic and disciplinary language. Students need this vocabulary to communicate their knowledge accurately and effectively within each subject (EEF, 2018).

HSC standards

Vocabulary is embedded in high achievement

NESA’s performance band descriptors, which inform the development of marking guidelines, associate higher achievement with the precise and appropriate use of disciplinary language.
