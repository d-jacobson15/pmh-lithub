Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Directive Decoder

Overview

A Directive Decoder is a Canvas page that breaks down the directive verbs students are expected to understand and respond to for a particular year level, subject or topic. It is an expanded glossary designed to clarify what exam-style questions require.

  • Definitions with student-friendly explanations of each verb’s requirements
  • Scaffolds to help students address the verb
  • Annotated exemplar responses that satisfy the verb
  • Annotated non-exemplars showing where the requirements have not been met
Why This Works

Graham and Perin’s (2007) meta-analysis of writing interventions for students in Years 4–12 found that explicit writing strategy instruction improved writing quality (d = 0.82), while studying models of good writing also had a positive effect (d = 0.25). Alfieri et al. (2013) found that comparing cases improved learning outcomes (d = 0.50), providing a rationale for examining annotated exemplars alongside non-exemplars. These results support underlying instructional practices rather than a Directive Decoder page specifically.

Implementation Sequence

Before Class

1

Identify the key directive verbs required for the unit of work. Ensure these align with HSC examination language.

2

Use the NESA Glossary of Key Words, past student exemplars and HSC marking guidelines to explain each verb in student-friendly language. Prepare relevant scaffolds, considerations or sentence starters.

3

Gather a range of student responses to the targeted verbs. Annotate where each response does or does not satisfy the verb.

4

Add the explanations, scaffolds and annotated responses to the Canvas page template and link it from the unit homepage.

5

Link the Decoder to a relevant classroom activity and require students to decode the verb before attempting the question.

During Class

1

Present an exam-style question. Ask students to identify its directive verb and use the Decoder page to review its definition, requirements and available scaffolds.

2

Compare the annotated exemplar and non-exemplar. Identify how the exemplar addresses the verb and where the non-exemplar falls short. Highlight relevant Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary.

3

Have students respond independently, applying the requirements of the directive verb and the features identified in the exemplar.

Implementation Advice

Identify Directive Verbs in Advance

Identify the verbs students will encounter before beginning the unit, aligning the Decoder with syllabus expectations, assessment tasks and relevant HSC-style questions.

Teach Directive Verbs in Context

Use authentic, subject-specific questions rather than isolated definitions so students understand what each verb requires in a written response.

Choose Exemplars Wisely

Select responses according to how well they address the directive verb, not simply their overall content quality. A response with weaker subject content may still demonstrate the thinking or structure required by the verb.

Revisit Directive Verbs Regularly

Embed the Decoder in everyday questions and tasks. Revisit each verb across different topics so students become more independent in applying its requirements.

Checking for Impact

  • Confirm the Decoder is available in the relevant Canvas unit and referenced during classroom tasks. Observe whether students consult it to interpret questions and plan responses.
  • Review students’ written work for evidence that they use the recommended scaffolds and response structures appropriately.
  • Track the percentage of students who address each targeted verb successfully in comparable assessments. Compare results over time, including independent responses, to evaluate whether understanding has transferred.

Across the Curriculum

Every KLA requires students to interpret directive verbs in Stage 6. Introduce the Decoder in Stages 4 and 5 so students can practise subject-specific thinking and response construction before the HSC.

Across subjects, use examples of increasingly sophisticated responses while retaining the precise expectations of each directive verb.
