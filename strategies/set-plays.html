Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Set Plays

Overview

Set plays are a series of graphic organisers that align to each directive verb. Used to plan students’ responses to a question, they aim to help students scaffold and develop thinking pathways to build familiarity with the requirements of a directive verb. After a student has completed the set play scaffold, they complete a full response to the given question.

Why This Works

Graham and Perin (2007) found that explicitly teaching students strategies for planning and constructing written responses had a large positive effect on writing quality (Effect Size = 0.82). The study also found positive effects for pre-writing activities, such as graphic organisers (Effect Size = 0.32).

Implementation Sequence

1

Select an academic task with a directive verb students need to familiarise themselves with.

2

Ask students to identify and discuss what the directive verb requires them to do. Use the NESA definition to clarify its purpose and expectations.

3

Provide students with the graphic organiser that corresponds to the directive verb. You could distribute printed copies to younger grades, or expect older grades to draw the scaffold themselves.

4

Have students use the graphic organiser to unpack the question, organise relevant knowledge and plan their response. Encourage students to draw on subject knowledge and available resources.

5

Students use their completed graphic organiser to construct a written response that addresses the requirements of the directive verb.

Implementation Advice

Gradually remove the scaffold

A common complication of this method is an overreliance on the scaffold. To prevent this, aim to use a gradual release from the scaffold. Once students have used the scaffold several times, and they have internalised the verb’s requirements, have them complete a question without it to help develop independent thought pathways.

Explicitly model the process

Explicit modelling helps students understand how to apply the requirements of a directive verb. Use the Gradual Release of Responsibility approach (I Do, We Do, You Do) with three different questions containing the same directive verb. This works particularly well with shorter responses. For extended responses, consider preparing a completed graphic organiser to demonstrate the planning process.

Start with familiar content

Use familiar subject content initially. When introducing a new directive verb, use questions involving content students already understand. This allows them to focus on developing the thinking and writing skills required by the verb without the additional challenge of learning unfamiliar subject knowledge.

Adapt set plays to your subject

The set plays in the ready-to-use resources are designed for a Science context. You may need to create your own subject-specific graphic organisers. Explicitly clarify to students that directive verbs can have different meanings and expectations across subjects, and model what each verb requires in your discipline.

Revisit directive verbs regularly

Rather than teaching each directive verb once, provide opportunities for students to apply the same verb across different topics and question types. This helps students recognise the underlying thinking process and transfer it to unfamiliar questions.

Checking for Impact

  • Assess Student Work: Improvements may become evident relatively quickly, particularly when students already have a strong understanding of the subject content. Rather than assessing what students know, focus on how effectively they communicate their knowledge in a way that meets the requirements of the directive verb.
  • Encourage Self-Assessment: Have students compare responses completed before and after using the scaffold. Encourage them to identify specific improvements in their expression, organisation of ideas and ability to address the requirements of the directive verb.
  • Use Pre- and Post-assessments: Have students complete an initial response without the scaffold, followed by a comparable response using it. Both should be completed under timed conditions to allow for meaningful comparison. A final independent response can then be used to assess whether these skills have transferred beyond the scaffold.
  • Track Specific Improvements: Use a simple checklist or marking criteria aligned with the directive verb to monitor student progress. For example, when assessing an evaluate response, consider whether students make a clear judgement, support it with relevant evidence and establish criteria for their judgement.
