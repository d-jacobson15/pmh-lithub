Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Visible Vocab

Overview

Visible Vocab is a strategy designed to strengthen students’ understanding and use of Tier 2 vocabulary. Tier 2 words are high-utility academic words that students encounter across different subjects and contexts, such as analyse, evaluate, significant, impact, demonstrate, justify and consequence.

Selected Tier 2 words are printed on Visible Vocab cards or posters, including a definition and/or a visual symbol, and displayed prominently around the classroom. Rather than functioning as passive displays, teachers repeatedly and explicitly direct students to the vocabulary during instruction, classroom discussion and writing activities, encouraging students to apply the words in context.

Why This Works

Through repeated exposure and active use across different learning contexts, students move from simply recognising and understanding academic vocabulary to confidently and independently using it in their speaking and writing. Vincy (2020) found that the influence of receptive vocabulary knowledge on students’ productive vocabulary knowledge increased from an average of 8% with conventional instruction to 72% with explicit instruction and repeated exposure, demonstrating a significant reduction in the gap between recognising and actively using vocabulary.

Implementation Sequence

1
Select the vocabulary

Before or during a unit of work, identify a small number of high-value Tier 2 words from the unit word list that students are likely to encounter and use repeatedly. Avoid displaying too many words at once. Visible Vocab should focus on words that students can realistically revisit across multiple lessons and contexts.

2
Display the words

Print the selected words using the Visible Vocab template linked on this page and place them in a prominent location in the classroom. Cards should be large enough to be easily seen and referred to from around the room.

3
Explicitly introduce the words

When a new word is added, briefly explain its meaning and model how it can be used in context. Students should hear the word used accurately before being expected to use it independently.

Teacher: “Our Visible Vocab word today is significant. Significant means important or having a noticeable effect. For example: Access to clean water has a significant impact on community health.”

4
Direct students to the Visible Vocab

Throughout the lesson, deliberately direct students’ attention back to the displayed vocabulary. This can occur during:

  • teacher explanation
  • questioning and classroom discussion
  • note-taking
  • modelled writing
  • short-answer responses
  • extended responses
  • peer feedback
  • lesson summaries and exit tickets

Teacher: “Look at our Visible Vocab. Which word could we use instead of important?”

Teacher: “Before you finish that paragraph, can you include one of our Visible Vocab words?”

Teacher: “Which Visible Vocab word would help us explain the relationship between these two ideas?”

5
Revisit and recycle

Keep words visible long enough for students to encounter and use them repeatedly. Deliberately revisit previously taught vocabulary so that students retrieve and apply it across different lessons and contexts.

Teacher: “Which word from our Visible Vocab last week could we use here?”

Over time, reduce teacher prompting as students begin to select and use appropriate academic vocabulary independently.

Implementation Advice

Keep it Visible

Place vocabulary where students can easily see and refer to it during normal classroom activities. Avoid placing the cards somewhere that becomes part of the background of the classroom.

Keep it Manageable

Display a small, purposeful collection of vocabulary rather than filling the classroom with words. Too many words can reduce students' ability to identify and recall the vocabulary that matters.

Refer Repeatedly

Displaying the vocabulary alone is not the strategy. The effectiveness of Visible Vocab comes from teachers repeatedly directing students to the words and creating opportunities for students to use them.

Use It Yourself

Teachers should deliberately model the words during explanations, questioning and classroom discussion. Students need to repeatedly hear, see and use the vocabulary.

Ensure Student Use

Move beyond asking students to define vocabulary. Prompt students to use words in a sentence, verbal response, short answer or extended response so that vocabulary becomes part of their working language.

Recycle Vocabulary

Do not immediately remove a word once a lesson or topic has finished. Revisit previously taught vocabulary and highlight when the same word appears in another context.

In Sum...

Keep vocabulary visible, manageable and regularly used so students can recall and apply it independently.

Checking for Impact

  • Self-Reflect: Reflect on your practice: are you regularly directing students to the Visible Vocab? Alternatively, ask a critical friend to attend your class and decide whether or not you are.
  • Check Student Work: Review students' books or assessment tasks for accurate and appropriate use of the displayed Tier 2 words. Look out for students independently referring to and using the vocabulary, both in written and spoken forms.
  • Writing Tasks: Complete a short writing task before and after implementing the strategy. Compare students’ independent and accurate use of Tier 2 vocabulary.
