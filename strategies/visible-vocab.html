Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Visible Vocab

Overview

Visible Vocab is a strategy designed to strengthen students’ understanding and use of Tier 2 vocabulary. Tier 2 words are high-utility academic words that students encounter across different subjects and contexts, such as analyse, evaluate, significant, impact, demonstrate, justify and consequence.

Selected Tier 2 words are printed on Visible Vocab cards or posters, including a definition and/or a visual symbol, and displayed prominently around the classroom. Rather than functioning as passive displays, teachers repeatedly and explicitly direct students to the vocabulary during instruction, classroom discussion and writing activities, encouraging students to apply the words in context.

Why This Works

Through repeated exposure and active use across different learning contexts, students move from simply recognising and understanding academic vocabulary to confidently and independently using it in their speaking and writing. Vincy (2020) found that the influence of receptive vocabulary knowledge on students’ productive vocabulary knowledge increased from an average of 8% with conventional instruction to 72% with explicit instruction and repeated exposure, demonstrating a significant reduction in the gap between recognising and actively using vocabulary.

Implementation Sequence

1

Identify an academic task where students’ responses would be strengthened by using more precise vocabulary.

2

Consider what a full-mark response would sound like. Select 2–3 Tier 2 words that you would expect to see used accurately.

3

Repeat the process for Tier 3 vocabulary, selecting 2–3 key subject-specific terms.

4

Copy the Visible Vocab template and add your selected words. When setting the task in Canvas, clearly state: “In your response, you must include the vocabulary specified in this criteria.”

Implementation Advice

Consistency

Reuse the same high-value words across lessons and tasks so students retrieve and apply them repeatedly.

Do not overload

Two to three Tier 2 words and two to three Tier 3 words per response is usually enough. Adjust this according to the mark value and complexity of the task.

Consider time

If students are expected to use the words accurately, they need time to learn them. Explicitly teach their meanings before requiring them in a response.

Restrict AI where appropriate

Students can easily ask generative AI to insert a word list into a response. Consider books-only application tasks or in-class writing.

In Sum...

Keep the list small, teach the words explicitly first and reuse them often enough that students begin to retrieve them independently.

Checking for Impact

  • Pre/Post Tests: Set a question at the beginning of the term, before introducing Visible Vocab. Collect a small sample of responses, scan them into AI and ask it to count the number of Tier 2 and Tier 3 words used accurately. Repeat the process at the end of the term and compare the change.
  • Vocabulary Tests: Use a short multiple-choice Google Form or paper-based quiz to check whether students have mastered the meaning and application of vocabulary included as part of the criteria throughout the unit.
  • Independent Transfer: Occasionally remove the Vocabulary Criteria for questions that require the use of previously learned vocabulary. Check whether students independently use previously taught Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary in their writing.
    • This can also be applied by exploring examination question responses to determine whether students are transferring vocabulary into unfamiliar tasks without prompting.
