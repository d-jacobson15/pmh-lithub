Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Vocabulary Cards

Overview

Vocabulary Cards connect familiar Tier 1 and lower-level Tier 2 words, such as important, big and show, with more precise academic alternatives. Each card presents a familiar central word alongside a small set of possible replacements. During a writing task, students use the cards to consider which word best conveys their intended meaning.

By building on words students already know, the strategy provides a manageable entry point into academic language. It supports more precise expression, explicit discussion of word choice and the transfer of vocabulary across learning areas.

Why This Works

The EEF’s Improving Literacy in Secondary Schools guidance recommends explicitly developing vocabulary across subject areas. The IES Improving Adolescent Literacy guide also recommends explicit vocabulary instruction, while its guidance for English learners recommends intensive teaching of selected academic words in multiple activities. These findings support the instructional principles behind Vocabulary Cards, although they do not establish an effect size for this exact card-based strategy.

Implementation Sequence

1
Select a writing activity

Choose an upcoming lesson, paragraph or extended response in which students would benefit from more precise academic vocabulary. Identify one or two high-frequency Tier 1 or low Tier 2 words students are likely to use and link the relevant Vocabulary Cards in the Canvas activity.

2
Introduce the card

Display a card. Explain that the central word is familiar vocabulary and the branches offer more precise Tier 2 alternatives. Discuss how meaning, subject and tone affect which alternative is appropriate.

3
Model word choice

Show a sentence from the lesson and think aloud as you choose a more accurate alternative. For example, “It is important to wear a seatbelt” becomes “It is vital to wear a seatbelt” when emphasising safety. Compare alternatives and explain why they are not always interchangeable.

4
Apply during writing

Give students access to the Canvas cards, adapted for the KLA. During the selected writing activity, prompt them to consult the cards when choosing words that convey their intended meaning.

5
Draft, then upgrade

Students draft their response first, then identify up to three general words to reconsider using the cards. They should keep the original word where it remains more accurate, rather than replacing it simply to sound sophisticated.

6
Justify and revisit

Ask students to explain one vocabulary choice to a partner or in a short exit ticket. Revisit the same target words in later writing tasks and gradually reduce prompts to check independent use.

Implementation Advice

Context determines the word

Synonyms are not interchangeable. Students should consider meaning, tone and discipline before selecting an alternative. “Vital” is a precise choice when a sentence concerns safety, while “momentous” implies far-reaching significance.

Avoid decorative substitutions

A more complex word is not necessarily better. Model the difference between accurate choices such as “demonstrates endurance” and awkward ones such as “evinces speed”.

Keep choices manageable

Limit each card to four to six useful alternatives. Introduce one or two target words per unit or writing task rather than overwhelming students.

Embed cards in drafting and editing

Treat the cards as a reference during authentic classroom writing, not as an isolated synonym drill. Use a “draft first, upgrade second” routine.

Differentiate deliberately

Provide sentence starters or simple visual cues where useful. Extend students with carefully selected Tier 3 vocabulary when subject knowledge makes that appropriate.

Checking for Impact

  • Compare student work: Review short responses before and after card use. Look for accurate, context-appropriate vocabulary and clearer expression, not simply a larger count of sophisticated words.
  • Use a one-minute exit ticket: Provide a familiar Tier 1 sentence from the lesson (for example, “The data shows a big difference”). Students select and justify a more precise alternative.
  • Check independent transfer: In later tasks and examinations, check whether students select appropriate vocabulary without the cards or explicit prompts.

Across the Curriculum

Illustrative vocabulary substitutions to adapt for your KLA. Select words according to disciplinary meaning and the specific question.

Mathematics

Before: The graph shows a steep rise in profits.

After: The graph illustrates a steep rise in profits.

Science

Before: Maintaining a stable internal environment is an important process for cell survival.

After: Maintaining a stable internal environment is a critical process for cell survival.

English

Before: The author shows the character’s internal struggle through symbolism.

After: The author reveals the character’s internal struggle through symbolism.

Visual Arts

Before: The contrast between light and dark creates a big impact.

After: The contrast between light and dark creates a profound impact.

Technologies

Before: User feedback is important when refining a prototype.

After: User feedback is pivotal when refining a prototype.

Music

Before: The final movement shows a change in dynamics.

After: The final movement demonstrates a change in dynamics.

HMS
