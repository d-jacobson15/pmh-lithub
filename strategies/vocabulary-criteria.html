Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Vocabulary Criteria

Overview

We often ask students to apply class content by answering questions or constructing written responses. Vocabulary Criteria ensure students use appropriate subject-specific terminology while also developing the more sophisticated academic language they need to communicate their ideas clearly. The strategy is simple: when setting a question or written response, provide students with a short list of Tier 2 and Tier 3 words that they are expected to use accurately in their response.

Why This Works

When students repeatedly encounter and use key vocabulary across lessons, and are held accountable for applying it, vocabulary growth improves. Quantifying this strategy, Townsend, Brock and Morrison (2018) reported effect sizes of 0.38 for general academic vocabulary and 0.45 for discipline-specific science vocabulary. Students’ average use of general academic words increased from 5.64 to 7.00, while discipline-specific words increased from 0.52 to 1.22.

Implementation Sequence

1

Identify an academic task where students’ responses would be strengthened by using more precise vocabulary.

2

Consider what a full-mark response would sound like. Select 2–3 Tier 2 words that you would expect to see used accurately.

3

Repeat the process for Tier 3 vocabulary, selecting 2–3 key subject-specific terms.

4

Copy the Vocabulary Criteria template and add your selected words. When setting the task in Canvas, clearly state: “In your response, you must include the vocabulary specified in this criteria.”

Implementation Advice

Consistency

Reuse the same high-value words across lessons and tasks so students retrieve and apply them repeatedly.

Do not overload

Two to three Tier 2 words and two to three Tier 3 words per response is usually enough. Adjust this according to the mark value and complexity of the task.

Consider time

If students are expected to use the words accurately, they need time to learn them. Explicitly teach their meanings before requiring them in a response.

Restrict AI where appropriate

Students can easily ask generative AI to insert a word list into a response. Consider books-only application tasks or in-class writing.

In Sum...

Keep the list small, teach the words explicitly first and reuse them often enough that students begin to retrieve them independently.

Checking for Impact

  • Pre/Post Tests: Set a question at the beginning of the term, before introducing Vocabulary Criteria. Collect a small sample of responses, scan them into AI and ask it to count the number of Tier 2 and Tier 3 words used accurately. Repeat the process at the end of the term and compare the change.
  • Vocabulary Tests: Use a short multiple-choice Google Form or paper-based quiz to check whether students have mastered the meaning and application of vocabulary included as part of the criteria throughout the unit.
  • Independent Transfer: Occasionally remove the Vocabulary Criteria for questions that require the use of previously learned vocabulary. Check whether students independently use previously taught Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary in their writing.
    • This can also be applied by exploring examination question responses to determine whether students are transferring vocabulary into unfamiliar tasks without prompting.

Extract Tier 2 & 3 Words with AI

Choose the approach that suits your planning. Copy the relevant prompt into your preferred AI tool, replace the bracketed details and review its suggestions against your students' needs and the unit outcomes.

Option 1: Extract words from a text or resource

Use this when you have an article, extract, worksheet, textbook passage or other source material.

I teach [year level and subject]. Our unit is [unit title]. Analyse the source material I provide below and identify 10 useful Tier 2 words (transferable academic vocabulary) and 10 Tier 3 words (specialist vocabulary specific to this subject or unit). Prioritise words that students need to understand the text, discuss its ideas and respond effectively to assessment tasks. Present your response as a two-column table headed “Tier 2 Vocabulary” and “Tier 3 Vocabulary”. For each word, provide a brief student-friendly definition and a sentence showing its use in this unit. Use only words that appear in or are directly supported by the source. If there are fewer than 10 suitable words in a tier, list fewer rather than inventing additional examples. Flag any words whose classification is context-dependent. SOURCE MATERIAL: [Paste the text or source material here]

Option 2: Generate a word list from a topic or syllabus

Use this when planning a new unit or identifying vocabulary from syllabus content points.

I teach [year level and subject]. Our topic/unit is [topic or unit name]. The relevant syllabus content points or learning outcomes are: [Paste syllabus points, outcomes or a brief topic description here] Recommend 10 Tier 2 words (transferable academic vocabulary used across subjects) and 10 Tier 3 words (specialist vocabulary needed to understand and communicate this topic). Prioritise words that will help students access the key concepts, read subject material and write or speak about the unit. Present the words in a two-column table headed “Tier 2 Vocabulary” and “Tier 3 Vocabulary”. Include a concise student-friendly definition and an example of each word in the unit's context. Base the selection on the syllabus points provided. Do not claim that a word is mandated by a syllabus unless it appears explicitly in the supplied text. Flag any uncertain classifications and suggest which words should be taught first, with a short reason.
