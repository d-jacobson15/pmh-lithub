Tier 2 & 3 Vocabulary

Word Protocols

Overview

Word Protocols are structured, step-by-step routines that help students decode, spell, understand, discuss and apply unfamiliar vocabulary. The Frayer Model, Word of the Day and Taboo each serve a different purpose: developing initial understanding, applying words in context and consolidating vocabulary through retrieval. Used purposefully within subject content, they help students develop more precise vocabulary and recognise when particular words are appropriate.

Why This Works

Research on vocabulary instruction supports combining explicit explanations with opportunities to process, discuss and use words in context. Stahl and Fairbanks (1986) reported positive effects of vocabulary instruction on comprehension, while Marzano’s work describes a structured approach involving explanation, student restatement, visual representation, practice, discussion and games. The reported effect sizes of 0.83 and 0.33 in the supplied draft have not been independently verified against the primary studies and are therefore not presented as confirmed effects of these particular protocols.

Introduce and deepen understanding

Implementation Sequence

  1. Select a relevant Tier 2 or Tier 3 word from the current unit.
  2. Distribute a Frayer Model and explicitly model the four sections: definition, characteristics, examples and non-examples.
  3. Students complete the organiser, discussing the word and constructing a definition in their own words.
  4. Compare students’ definitions with a reliable reference and revise any misconceptions.

Implementation Advice

  • Best used when introducing important new vocabulary. Model the process before independent practice.
  • Adapt the quadrants to suit the discipline. For some words, context, word families or applications may work better than non-examples.
  • Revisit the completed organiser in a later task to consolidate understanding.

Checking for Impact

  • Use a brief pre/post explanation or concept check to assess whether students can explain the word accurately.
  • Ask students to identify a new example and explain why a non-example does not fit.

Apply vocabulary in context

Implementation Sequence

  1. Choose a Tier 2 or Tier 3 word that directly supports the lesson’s content, text or writing activity.
  2. Display the word, a student-friendly definition and a subject-specific example sentence in the Canvas announcement or classwork, near the learning intentions.
  3. Briefly model the meaning and discuss when the word would be appropriate.
  4. Build an opportunity to use the word into an existing discussion or writing task, then review its accuracy.

Implementation Advice

  • Plan target words in advance so they align with unit content and lesson outcomes.
  • Avoid an isolated daily word list: every word should have a purposeful application within the lesson.
  • Revisit words from earlier lessons and gradually remove prompts.

Checking for Impact

  • Collect short written responses to check whether students use the word accurately in context.
  • Look for unprompted use of previously taught words in later discussions and assessments.

Retrieve and explain vocabulary

Implementation Sequence

  1. Select previously taught Tier 2 or Tier 3 terms that students need to recall and understand.
  2. Prepare cards with the target word at the top and three to five prohibited clue words beneath it.
  3. In pairs or small groups, one student explains the target without saying the word or any prohibited terms while others guess.
  4. Rotate roles and cards, then discuss which clues demonstrated the most accurate understanding.

Implementation Advice

  • Best used after vocabulary has been explicitly taught, particularly for revision and consolidation.
  • Allow confident students to create their own cards, selecting prohibited words that make explanations more demanding.
  • Set clear group expectations and prioritise accurate explanations over guessing speed.

  • Listen for increasingly precise explanations and students’ ability to describe terms without obvious associated words.
  • Use a follow-up independent definition or application question to verify understanding, rather than relying on game scores alone.

Other Word Protocols

P+5 — Visualising and extending word meaning

Students draw a picture representing a target word and identify five examples, related words or synonyms. Check that the choices accurately reflect the word’s meaning.

Text Rendering — Collaborative meaning-making

Students select significant words and phrases from a reading, discuss their interpretations and collaboratively clarify or extend the text’s meaning.
