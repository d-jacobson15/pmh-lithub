Word Protocols

Overview

Word protocols are a range of different methods that can be used to introduce new words to students. They should be clearly structured and utilise step-by-step routines in order to teach students how to decode, spell, understand, and discuss words and texts. There are a number of different protocols: the ones detailed on this page are the Frayer Model, Word of the Day activities and Taboo. Protocols such as these widen the range of vocabulary students are utilising, while helping them understand the appropriate contexts within which they can be used.

Why This Works

According to Marzano (2004), direct vocabulary instruction using structured approaches (including graphic organisers such as the Frayer Model) has a large effect on comprehension (d = 0.83). The six-step vocabulary process (description, restatement, visual, activities, discussion, games) is more effective than definition-only approaches. Additionally, Stahl & Fairbanks (1986) found that mixed methods which combine definitions with contextual usage and active processing (including graphic organisers) produce stronger vocabulary gains (d = 0.33 for comprehension) than definition-only or context-only approaches. Deeper processing of word meaning leads to better retention and transfer.

Introduce and deepen understanding

Implementation Sequence

  1. Select a Tier 2/3 word that you are teaching students
  2. Provide students with a Frayer Model template and explicitly walk them through the process of analysing the word to determine its meaning. Students: write the word, write the characteristics of the word, write examples of the selected word, write non-examples of the selected word and write a definition of the word in their own language.

Implementation Advice

  • This strategy is best used to explicitly teach Tier 2 and 3 words
  • The ‘examples and non-examples’ may not work for every discipline, but you may tweak the four spaces to suit your discipline (e.g. drawing a picture, writing down common confusions or using it in different sentences.
  • Words introduced using the model should be revisited. Consider placing larger exemplar diagrams around the classroom, asking students to refer back to their defined words as they complete work or using other protocols such as Taboo to retrieve words and embed them into long-term memory.

Apply vocabulary in context

Implementation Sequence

  1. Selects a Tier 2 or 3 word for the lesson, based on the content that will be covered. Ensure the word is one that you would expect students to use when writing about the lesson content
  2. Include the word on the on the announcement (this could go underneath learning intentions or in classwork). Ideally, include a definition of the word and a contextual sentence.
  3. Decide on an activity that students can embed the word into. This could be in an already planned activity, or you could specifically plan a writing task that students could do that utilises the word.

Implementation Advice

  • This strategy works well to explicitly teach Tier 2 and 3 words. It is most effective when it the word chosen is connected to relevant content and, importantly, is revisited throughout the year.
  • This strategy requires you to be organised with a word of the day for each lesson! It might be worthwhile planning your words in advance. AI can help you to extract relevant words from source texts, or you can consult your unit vocabulary list.
  • Avoid turning this into a stereotypical vocabulary list activity! Students should embed the word into their writing rather than merely create a list of definitions at the back of their workbook.

Retrieve and explain vocabulary

Implementation Sequence

  1. Create cards with a key term (Tier 2 or 3) at the top of the card. Below it, include 3-5 ‘taboo’ words.
  2. Students will pair up or form small groups. The selected student describes the top key term to their team, without using any of the ‘taboo’ words to do so.
  3. The team guesses the term based on the description provided.
  4. Rotate with different words/groups.

Implementation Advice

  • This strategy is best used AFTER Tier 2/3 words have been learnt, to recall them and ensure that students know their meaning.
  • To differentiate for higher ability students, get them to help with creating some of the taboo cards. You could get students to make these cards themselves as an activity and get them to run it together!
  • This is a strong example of how gamification can be used to enhance learning, but needs to be managed well. If you are working in a class of 60, consider running this activity with 20 or so of them in a breakout space.
  • This has been effective in the past when used as a revision strategy close to exams and assessments.

Checking for Impact

1. Check students’ understanding of target words

Before introducing a word, ask students to explain its meaning or use it in a sentence. Repeat this after the protocol, checking whether students can provide an accurate definition, distinguish examples from non-examples and explain the word without relying on the original scaffold or clues.

2. Monitor vocabulary use in classroom responses

Review student discussion and written work for evidence that explicitly taught Tier 2 and Tier 3 words are being used accurately and appropriately. Compare short responses over time, focusing on the precision and context of word choices rather than simply counting new vocabulary.

3. Assess independent retrieval and transfer

Revisit target words in a later lesson through a brief retrieval question, explanation task or unfamiliar example without displaying the original word supports. Monitor whether students recall meanings more readily, explain concepts with greater confidence and independently apply the vocabulary across new tasks or topics.

Other examples of Word Protocols

P+5

Students draw a picture representing a selected word and list five examples or synonyms beneath it. They explain how each choice connects to the word’s meaning.

Word Webs

Students create a visual network connecting a target word to related concepts, synonyms, antonyms and examples. They explain the relationships between the branches to deepen their understanding.

Vocabulary Continuum

Students position a word between two opposing concepts on a continuum. They justify its placement by considering the word’s meaning and degree of intensity.

Context Detective

Students infer the meaning of an unfamiliar word from several sentences before receiving its formal definition. They identify the contextual clues that informed their interpretation and compare their inference with the definition.

Odd One Out

Students identify which of four words does not belong with the others. They justify their selection by explaining the meanings and relationships between the words.

Reverse Dictionary

Students receive a definition, example or scenario and retrieve the corresponding academic term. They explain which clues helped them identify the correct word.

Vocabulary Pictionary

Students draw a visual representation of previously taught vocabulary while classmates attempt to identify the term. They discuss which features of the drawing communicate the meaning most effectively.

Vocabulary Charades

Students physically demonstrate a previously taught word or concept without speaking while peers identify it. Students explain the clues that helped them retrieve the term.

Year 10 English: P+5 Example

Students are introduced to a different target word each lesson through a classroom announcement.

Students then create a P+5 diagram and use the new word at least three times during the lesson. They must also use a word introduced in a previous lesson at least once. As an exit ticket, teachers conduct spot checks in which students identify where they have used the target words.
