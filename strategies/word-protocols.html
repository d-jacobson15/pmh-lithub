Word Protocols

Overview

Word protocols are a range of different methods that can be used to introduce new words to students. They should be clearly structured and utilise step-by-step routines in order to teach students how to decode, spell, understand, and discuss words and texts. There are a number of different ways these can be implemented, including the Frayer Model, constructing a P+5 diagram, Taboo, Word of the Day, etc.

This addresses our literacy area of focus by widening the range of vocabulary students are utilising, but also understanding the appropriate contexts with which they can be used. These contexts can be both in general as well as in subject-specific areas and text types.

Supporting Evidence:

Direct vocabulary instruction using structured approaches (including graphic organisers like the Frayer Model) produces large effects on comprehension (d = 0.83). The six-step vocabulary process (description, restatement, visual, activities, discussion, games) is more effective than definition-only approaches.

Marzano, R. J. (2004) Building Background Knowledge for Academic Achievement: Research on What Works in Schools)

Mixed methods that combine definitions with contextual usage and active processing (including graphic organisers) produce stronger vocabulary gains (d = 0.33 for comprehension) than definition-only or context-only approaches. Deeper processing of word meaning leads to better retention and transfer.

(Stahl, S. A., & Fairbanks, M. M. (1986) The Effects of Vocabulary Instruction: A Model-Based Meta-Analysis)

For example, The Frayer Model is a four-square graphic organiser, for vocabulary. It uses definition, characteristics, examples and non-examples (though these can be adjusted depending on your discipline). This helps learners build vocabulary, according to Frayer (1969).

Implementation Sequence

Some examples of word protocols and how you can add them into your lesson..

Introduce and deepen understanding

Implementation Sequence

How to implement the protocol

  1. Select a Tier 2/3 word that you are teaching students
  2. Provide students with a Frayer Model template and explicitly walk them through the process of analysing the word to determine its meaning. Students:
  3. Write the word
  4. Write the characteristics of the word.
  5. Write the examples of the selected word.
  6. Write non-examples of the selected word.
  7. Write a definition of the word in your own words.
  8. Check the meaning with dictionary definition

Implementation Advice

  • Best used to explicitly teach Tier 2/3 words
  • The ‘examples and non-examples’ may not work for every discipline, but you may tweak the model to suit your discipline (see example above)
  • Frayer's Model helps learners brainstorm project ideas. Teachers use it after projects to reinforce learning. This method effectively supports each learner's knowledge retention.

Checking for Impact

  • Students should be able to learn and understand new concepts/key terms more quickly in this manner. It should also impact students' ability to explain and recall these terms more frequently as a result.
  • Teachers can monitor the effect of this strategy through simple diagnostic and formative assessment in how students are able to understand new key terms.

Apply vocabulary in context

Implementation Sequence

How to implement the protocol

  1. Teacher selects a Tier 2 or 3 word for the lesson. Choose this based on the context of your lesson.
  2. Set this on the announcement - this could go underneath learning intentions or in classwork. Include a definition of the word and a contextual sentence.
  3. Decide on an activity that students can embed the word into. This could be in an already planned activity, or you could specifically plan a writing task that students could do that utilises the word.

Implementation Advice

  • Best used to explicitly teach Tier 2/3 words
  • Requires you to be organised with a word of the day for each lesson! Might be worthwhile planning in advance.
  • Avoid turning this into a stereotypical vocabulary list activity!

Checking for Impact

  • Students should also be able to learn and understand new terms more quickly.
  • Students' vocabulary should widen as a result of utilising this activity on an ongoing basis.
  • Teachers can monitor the effect of this strategy by observing how students use a wider range of vocabulary in their written responses.

Retrieve and explain vocabulary

Implementation Sequence

How to implement the protocol

  1. Create cards with a key term (Tier 2 or 3) at the top of the card. Below it, include 3-5 ‘taboo’ words.
  2. Students will pair up/in small groups
  3. The selected student describes the top key term to their team, and are not allowed to use any of the ‘taboo’ words to do so.
  4. The team guesses the term based on the description provided.
  5. Rotate with different words/groups.

Implementation Advice

  • Best used AFTER Tier 2/3 words have been learnt
  • To differentiate for higher ability students, get them to help with creating some of the taboo cards
  • A good example of gamification of learning, but needs to be managed well
  • You could get students to make these cards themselves as an activity and get them to run it together!
  • May be best used as a revision strategy

Checking for Impact

  • Students will be able to develop an understanding of the term by being able to explain/understand what the term being referred to is with uncommon words. Forces students to think outside the box in their explanations, improving their active recall.
  • Teachers can monitor the effectiveness of this strategy over time by observing how easily/quickly students are able to identify the ‘taboo’ word. They can also observe how students are able to describe these terms in depth to the student that is guessing.
